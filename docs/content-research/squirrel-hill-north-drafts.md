# Stroll: draft texts for north Squirrel Hill

First drafts of the **prompt**, **reveal**, **story lines** and **alt text** for 8 places in
north Squirrel Hill: Fifth Avenue to Forbes Avenue, from Schenley Park east to about Beechwood
Boulevard. Drafted November 20, 2026, for proofreading. Same format and style as
`stroll-40-drafts.md`.

**How these were written**
- Every fact was found online on November 20, and the sources are linked under each place.
  Where sources disagree, the draft avoids the disputed detail, and a **Note** says so.
- **⚠ check** marks something to confirm on site before recording.
- Lengths follow the location research guide: prompt up to about 25 words, reveal one sentence,
  story lines up to about 30 words, written for the ear. Prompts describe the detail rather than
  saying "look" or "spot".
- The mix is deliberate: a few well-known stops (the Manor, Chatham) plus smaller
  local ones (a cobblestone street, a park, the shopping corner).
- **Alt text describes the chosen photo** (see **Photo** under each place). Places without a
  photo yet keep alt text written from descriptions, to rewrite once we have one.

### 1. Manor Theatre · Architecture

**Why:** A well-known neighborhood landmark with a surprising answer, and it's still in use.
- **Prompt:** This building on Murray Avenue has timber beams set into its walls, like an old English cottage. What do you think has been inside it for over a hundred years?
- **Reveal:** A movie theater: the Manor has shown films here since 1922.
- **Story 1:** It opened on May 15, 1922, with a silent film called *Hail the Woman*.
- **Story 2:** The Tudor look was chosen to blend in with the houses nearby, so a movie theater could pass for a manor house.
- **Story 3:** From the 1930s on, Squirrel Hill had at least three movie theaters for forty years. Today the Manor is the one that's left.
- **Alt text:** A two-story corner building with timber beams set into pale walls and steep brown roofs, with a tall MANOR sign and a marquee over the entrance.
- **Photo:** `manor-theatre.jpg`, the whole building. [Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Manor_Theatre,_Squirrel_Hill,_2024-12-06.jpg) · Cbaile19 · CC0. The MANOR sign and marquee are visible, so the photo hints at the answer.
- **Sources:** [WESA, 2022](https://wesa.fm/arts-sports-culture/2022-05-11/marquee-name-pittsburghs-oldest-movie-theater-celebrates-a-century-of-film-with-a-festival) · [TribLive](https://triblive.com/aande/movies-tv/manor-theatre-celebrates-a-century-in-squirrel-hill) · [Squirrel Hill Historical Society: "Squirrel Hill goes to the movies"](https://squirrelhillhistory.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/08/squirrel-hill-goes-to-the-movies.pdf) · [Father Pitt](https://fatherpitt.com/2024/12/07/squirrel-hills-manor-has-the-atmosphere-of-a-country-club/)
- **Note:** News stories call it Pittsburgh's oldest continuously operating movie theater. That's left out because a TribLive headline mentions the Manor *reopening*, so it may have closed for a while, and the claim is hard to check. Sources also disagree on the original number of seats.
- ⚠ **Check:** the exterior has been simplified over the years. Confirm the timber beams are still visible before recording the prompt.

### 2. Forbes and Murray ("upstreet") · History

**Why:** The heart of the neighborhood. A local stop that explains the name Squirrel Hill.
- **Prompt:** Two busy streets cross here, lined with bakeries, delis and shops. Locals have a nickname for this shopping strip. What do you think they call it?
- **Reveal:** "Upstreet", short for "up the street", because this corner is the heart of Squirrel Hill.
- **Story 1:** It took off after electric trolleys came up Forbes and Murray in 1893. The first shops on record opened around 1911.
- **Story 2:** Jewish merchants were here from the very first store listings. By the 1950s, the strip was known for its fashion shops.
- **Story 3:** And the name Squirrel Hill? It most likely comes from the many black squirrels that once lived here.
- **Alt text:** A modern library with a tall glass front on a busy street corner, with cars parked along the curb and a bus going by.
- **Photo:** `forbes-and-murray.jpg`, the library on the corner. [Flickr](https://www.flickr.com/photos/44124427152@N01/60677048) · Salim Virji · CC BY-SA 2.0. It's from Flickr, not Commons, and about 20 years old, so the cars and bus look a little dated. Swap it for our own photo if we take one.
- **Sources:** [Squirrel Hill Historical Society: development of the business district](https://squirrelhillhistory.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/05/the-development-of-squirrel-hille28099s-business-district.pdf) · [Squirrel Hill (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squirrel_Hill) · [Rauh Jewish Archives](https://rauhjewisharchives.org/entry/squirrel-hill) · [WITF: how Squirrel Hill got its name](https://witf.org/2022/07/29/how-did-pittsburghs-squirrel-hill-neighborhood-get-its-name)
- **Note:** The name's origin isn't documented. Sources say "most likely" squirrels, so the line says that too. The Historical Society gives both 1911 and 1912 for the first shop, so the line says "around 1911". Pin it at the Carnegie Library branch on the northwest corner, which has stood there since 1972.

### 3. Temple Sinai (Worthington Mansion) · Architecture

**Why:** A grand house with a second life, and an easy stop on Forbes Avenue.
- **Prompt:** This big mansion on Forbes Avenue was built in 1914 for an oil company executive. What do you think it has been since the 1940s?
- **Reveal:** A synagogue: Temple Sinai, a Reform congregation founded in 1946, made the mansion its home.
- **Story 1:** It was built for John Worthington, who died in 1918, just a few years after it was finished.
- **Story 2:** The house is about 23,000 square feet. Even so, the congregation added more space over the years.
- **Story 3:** It was named a Pittsburgh historic landmark in 1976.
- **Alt text:** A stone mansion with tall chimneys and leaded windows, behind a curving path, old trees and autumn leaves.
- **Photo:** `temple-sinai-worthington-mansion.jpg`. [Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Worthington_Mansion_exterior_2.jpg) · Sdkb · CC BY-SA 4.0. The photo answers part of the check below: the house is stone.
- **Sources:** [Historical marker (HMdb)](https://www.hmdb.org/m.asp?m=263625) · [Temple Sinai: our history](https://www.templesinaipgh.org/about-us/our-history) · [Rauh Jewish Archives: Worthington estate](https://rauhjewisharchives.org/photos/john-worthington-estate-photograph/)
- **Note:** Sources disagree on whether the congregation bought the house in 1947 or 1949, so the reveal says "since the 1940s". In 2025, Temple Sinai and Rodef Shalom (already in the app) were exploring a merger, so check the building's current use before recording.
- ⚠ **Check:** the building's material and what can be seen from the sidewalk.

### 4. Sunnyledge · Architecture

**Why:** A striking corner tower with a story you'd never guess from the sidewalk.
- **Prompt:** At one corner of this house, the wall curves out into a big round tower with a cone-shaped roof. What do you think that room was used for?
- **Reveal:** It was a doctor's office: Dr. James McClelland saw patients there, and they came in through their own porch.
- **Story 1:** McClelland was a homeopathic doctor to Andrew Carnegie and the Frick and Mellon families, and he founded nearby Shadyside Hospital.
- **Story 2:** Boston architects designed it in 1886, while they were also finishing a famous house in Chicago. The wooden spindles for both houses were made at the same time.
- **Story 3:** His younger daughter lived here for 94 years. Today it's a small hotel and tea room.
- **Alt text:** A large red brick house with a round corner tower under a cone-shaped slate roof, a tall chimney, and a lawn sloping down to the street.
- **Photo:** `sunnyledge.jpg`. [Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Sunnyledge,_Squirrel_Hill,_Pittsburgh,_2021-10-07.jpg) · Cbaile19 · CC0. A [2016 photo by Dllu](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Sunnyledge,_Pittsburgh,_2016.jpg) (CC BY-SA 4.0) shows the tower from closer, if we want a second choice.
- **Sources:** [Sunnyledge (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunnyledge) · [SAH Archipedia: Sunnyledge Hotel](https://sah-archipedia.org/node/16963)
- **Note:** Some sources place it in Shadyside, but the city lists it in Squirrel Hill North. The architects were Longfellow, Alden and Harlow, and the Chicago house is the Glessner House.
- ⚠ **Check:** that the tower is on the corner you can see from the sidewalk (Archipedia says the northeast corner).

### 5. Andrew Mellon Hall, Chatham University · History

**Why:** A famous name and a surprising detail. It also echoes the Guastavino tile in the Rodef Shalom stop.
- **Prompt:** In 1917, banker Andrew Mellon bought this mansion and more than doubled its size. One addition was rare in a home back then. What do you think it was?
- **Reveal:** An indoor swimming pool, 60 feet long and lined with Guastavino tile.
- **Story 1:** The house was first built for George Laughlin, of the Jones and Laughlin steel family.
- **Story 2:** Mellon also added a bowling alley, tennis courts and gardens. Around 1940, his family gave the house to the women's college that became Chatham University.
- **Story 3:** The pool closed in 2004 and is now a board room. The university president's office is in this building.
- **Alt text:** A large red brick Tudor-style mansion with three pointed gables, tall chimneys and a carved stone doorway, behind trimmed shrubs.
- **Photo:** `andrew-mellon-hall.jpg`. [Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Mellon_Hall,_Chatham_University,_2023-04-20,_01.jpg) · Cbaile19 · CC0. The same photographer has [interior photos](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Mellon_Hall_(Chatham_University)), including the grand staircase, if we want one for Story 3.
- **Sources:** [PHLF: "College eyes pool"](https://phlf.org/college-eyes-pool/) · [Chatham: Mellon Board Room](https://events.chatham.edu/mellon-board-room) · [Chatham University (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_University)
- **Note:** Sources disagree on when the house was built (1887, 1897 or 1902) and whether the gift was in 1940 or 1941, so the lines avoid exact dates.
- ⚠ **Check:** whether walkers may enter campus here, or whether the stop should be heard from Woodland Road.

### 6. Chatham University Arboretum · Natural history

**Why:** A natural history stop in a mostly built-up area. It's short, and that's fine.
- **Prompt:** The old trees and lawns around you were once one man's private estate. Today the whole campus counts as something special. What do you think it is?
- **Reveal:** An arboretum: 32 acres of campus recognized as a living collection of trees.
- **Story 1:** It was Andrew Mellon's estate. Parts of the grounds were designed in the 1930s by the Olmsted Brothers, the firm run by the sons of Central Park's designer.
- **Story 2:** The campus was named an arboretum in 1998.
- **Alt text:** Sunlight through the branches of tall old trees over a wide, shady lawn, with a house roof in the distance.
- **Photo:** `chatham-university-arboretum.jpg`. [Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Chatham_University_Arboretum_-_IMG_7655.JPG) · Daderot · public domain.
- **Sources:** [Chatham University Arboretum (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_University_Arboretum) · [Chatham Arboretum](https://chatham.edu/locations/shadyside-campus/arboretum.html)
- **Note:** This is close to #5. If both feel like too much Chatham, keep one, or fold Story 1 here into #5.

### 7. Murray Hill Avenue · History

**Why:** A small local gem: you hear it and feel it before you read about it.
- **Prompt:** Listen to the cars, or to your own footsteps, on this street. What do you think it's paved with, and why wasn't it covered over?
- **Reveal:** Cobblestones: when the city planned to pave over them, neighbors worked with their city councilman to keep them.
- **Story 1:** The street is named for the estate of James B. Murray. Until about 1891, it was called Ardsheil Avenue.
- **Story 2:** It has a Latin nickname: rus in urbe, "a bit of country in the city."
- **Story 3:** The house at number 1171 is believed to be the first one built here. The street became a city historic district in 2000.
- **Alt text:** A quiet, gently sloping cobblestone street lined with large old houses and tall trees.
- **Photo:** none found. Take our own on the site visit: a low angle along the cobblestones works best.
- **Sources:** [Murray Hill Historic District booklet (2000)](https://youngpreservationists.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Murray-Hill-Historic-District.pdf) · [Pittsburgh streets: Murray Hill Avenue](https://zifyoip.com/pittsburgh-streets/Murray_Hill_Avenue) · [City historic designations](https://www.pittsburghpa.gov/Business-Development/City-Planning/Historic-Preservation/City-Historic-Designations)
- **Note:** The councilman was Dan Cohen. His name is left out to keep the reveal short.
- ⚠ **Check:** that the cobblestones are still there along the stretch where we place the pin.

### 8. Wightman Park sculptures · Public art

**Why:** A neighborhood park whose art quietly tells its history. It pairs well with Wightman School, which is already in the app.
- **Prompt:** These sculptures have colorful glass panes shaped like butterflies, bugs and plants. Why do you think the artists chose glass for this park?
- **Reveal:** This land was once home to glass factories, including Thomas Wightman's glassworks.
- **Story 1:** Artists Oreen Cohen and Alison Zapata based the designs on drawings by children from a nearby children's center.
- **Story 2:** The glass was made by Jeannette Specialty Glass, a western Pennsylvania glass maker.
- **Story 3:** The park reopened in 2020 with a slide down the bank from Solway Street and a pond that soaks up rainwater.
- **Alt text:** Outdoor sculptures with bright panes of colored glass in the shapes of insects and leaves, beside a park path.
- **Photo:** none found. Take our own on the site visit, close enough to show the glass shapes.
- **Sources:** [TribLive: new sculptures at Wightman Park](https://triblive.com/local/new-sculptures-are-part-of-improvements-to-squirrel-hllls-wightman-park) · [Pittsburgh City Paper, 2020](https://pghcitypaper.com/news/wightman-park-reopens-with-new-inclusive-playgrounds-sustainable-infrastructure-and-public-art-18266220) · [Kidsburgh](https://kidsburgh.org/?p=72483)
- **Note:** The children's center is the Carriage House Children's Center. Its name is left out of the line to keep it short.
- ⚠ **Check:** that the sculptures are still in place, which shapes they show, and where they stand in the park, for the pin.
